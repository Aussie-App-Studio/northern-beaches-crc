Our Beliefs & Confessions

Rooted in the infallible Word of God, summarised in the Five Solas, the Doctrines of Grace, and our historic Reformed confessions.

Core Christian Faith

What We Believe

Nine core convictions that shape our preaching, worship, and daily discipleship on the Northern Beaches.

01

The Authority of the Bible

We believe that the Holy Bible, both the Old and New Testaments, are the infallible Word of God. We believe that God has spoken with authority and in a reliable way through the Bible. In our church community we acknowledge the Bible to be the rule for our faith and behaviour. Through the work of the Holy Spirit, the Word of God is able to make all people wise unto salvation. Copies of the Bible and the Heidelberg Catechism are available upon request.

02

The Centrality of Christ

The Bible points people to Jesus Christ. He died a cruel death on a cross for the sins of all those who place their trust and hope in Him and has been resurrected and exalted to reign over the universe. In our church community we proclaim Jesus to be the Lord and Saviour. We teach that ultimately no one can come to God and hence eternal life, except through Jesus Christ.

03

The Activity of the Holy Spirit

People will only come to faith in Jesus Christ by the work of God’s Holy Spirit. He convinces people of their sin and need for forgiveness through the work of Jesus as the perfect sacrifice to pay for their sin. The Holy Spirit produces thankfulness in the believer’s heart and changes a person’s life towards increasing obedience to God until He takes us to be with Him in heaven.

04

The Universality of the Church

God’s people are not confined to one denomination, one nation or one time. Every person who serves Jesus as Lord by the Holy Spirit is a son or daughter of God, the Father. In our church community we welcome people who love the Lord.

05

The Command of the Gospel

The Gospel (Good News) of God in Jesus Christ must be preached in Dee Why as well as on the Northern Beaches. We believe that the Holy Spirit is able to convict people of their sin and cause them to turn from their sins and believe in Jesus Christ as their Lord and Saviour. In our church community we call people to faith and send people into the world to proclaim this Gospel.

06

The Necessity of Obedience

God calls people to believe in Jesus so that they may live a life of holiness and love to the glory of God. In our church community, we urge each other to do the good works that God has intended us to do.

07

The Certainty of Eternal Life

A person who believes in Jesus Christ has been ‘born again’ and is therefore destined for eternal life. Such a person is delivered from the meaninglessness of life and from the eternal punishment of hell, adopted by God as His child, indwelt by the Holy Spirit, and assured of a home in heaven. In our church community we rejoice in this certain hope of life forever with our God.

08

The Effectiveness of the Sacraments

Jesus has told people to be baptised in His Name and to remember Him at the Lord’s Supper. In our church community we receive these signs of God’s love with faith and joy to strengthen us for obedience.

09

The Simplicity of Worship

God is pleased when people respond to His Word with joyful hearts and obedient service. In our church community we seek to make God’s message as plain as possible and to encourage a response of gratitude and obedience.

Our Calling & Signposts

The 5 Loves of Our Church

As a church we must ask ourselves: what are we here to do? What is our purpose and how do we fulfil that purpose? Jesus gives us insight into the life we are called to live in Matthew 22:35–40:

“Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind. This is the greatest and most important command. The second is like it: Love your neighbor as yourself. All the Law and the Prophets depend on these two commands.” Matthew 22:37–40

This helps us identify five key loves that we hope will define the life of our church — five signposts of our purpose and reminders of what we are called to do and be as disciples of Jesus.

1. The Love of God

The love of God is where Jesus began, and it is where we too must start when we consider what the purpose of the church is. Without the love of God all else fails, which is why Jesus said: “Love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind.” — Matthew 22:37

2. The Love of the Word

For us to properly love God, we must know Him. For us to serve God, we must understand His will. We are utterly dependent on His Word for both, and hence our love of God’s Word is the natural result of our love of God: “Your word is a lamp for my feet, a light on my path.” — Psalm 119:105

3. The Love for the Lost and Vulnerable

Throughout the Bible we see God seeking out the lost and the vulnerable. His care and attention are often focused on those who either need to be drawn into relationship with Him or those who are in grave personal need: “For the Son of Man has come to seek and to save the lost.” — Luke 19:10

4. The Love of Service

To love someone is more than simply feeling nice things about them. Love requires action; love requires service. In serving each other we not only love each other, but we love God in the process: “For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many.” — Mark 10:45

5. The Love of the Church

The church, God’s chosen people, is the object of God’s greatest love. It was for the church that Jesus lived, died and rose. Therefore, if God loves the church that much then we should love the church also: “But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his possession, so that you may proclaim the praises of the one who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.” — 1 Peter 2:9

Reformation Bedrock

The Five Solas

Five foundational declarations of the Protestant Reformation that point us purely to God’s grace in Christ.

Sola Gratia & Christus

1. Christ Alone

John 3:16–17: “For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life. For God did not send his Son into the world to condemn the world, but to save the world through him.”

Acts 4:12: “Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given to men by which we must be saved.”

Sola Scriptura

2. Scripture Alone

2 Timothy 3:15–17: “And how from infancy you have known the Scriptures, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus. All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the man of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work.”

Sola Fide

3. Faith Alone

Romans 3:21–28: “This righteousness from God comes through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe... For we maintain that a man is justified by faith apart from observing the law.”

Hebrews 11:1: “Now faith is being sure of what we hope for and certain of what we do not see.”

Sola Gratia

4. Grace Alone

Ephesians 2:8–9: “For it is by grace you have been saved, through faith — and this not from yourselves, it is the gift of God — not by works, so that no one can boast.”

Soli Deo Gloria

5. Glory to God Alone

Many passages in Scripture call on all creation to give all glory to God alone — see Exodus 14:4ff; Psalm 8:1; 19:1; 24:7ff; 29:1ff; 57:1ff; 73:25–28; 1 Corinthians 10:31; Romans 11:36; Revelation 1:6; and Revelation 4:11.

The Five Points of Calvinism

The Doctrines of Grace (T.U.L.I.P.)

Defined by the Synod of Dort (1618–1619) in response to the five Remonstrance points of Arminian teaching, the Doctrines of Grace affirm that salvation from start to finish belongs to our sovereign, merciful God.

In our natural state of spiritual death, we are unable and unwilling to come to Christ.

  • Jeremiah 17:9: “The heart is deceitful above all things, and beyond cure.”
  • Matthew 15:19: “For out of the heart come evil thoughts, murder, adultery, sexual immorality, theft, false testimony, slander.”
  • Romans 3:10–18: “As it is written: There is no one righteous, not even one; there is no one who understands, no one who seeks God.”

God’s choice in salvation is not dependent on human will or ability, or any foreseen traits.

  • Ephesians 1:4–5: “Before the foundations of the world… adopted as sons and daughters.”
  • Romans 5:8 & 10: “…While we were still sinners, Christ died for us… when we were God’s enemies, we were reconciled to him through the death of his Son.”
  • Romans 8:30: “And those he predestined he also called; those he called he also justified; those he justified, he also glorified.”

Christ fully paid the sin debt of His people and accomplished salvation on their behalf.

  • Acts 13:48: “…and all who were appointed for eternal life believed.”
  • Romans 9:13 & 15: “Just as it is written: ‘Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated.’ … ‘I will have mercy on whom I have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I have compassion.’”
  • 2 Corinthians 6:2 & John 17:20: “My prayer is not for them alone. I pray also for those who will believe in me through their message…”

Those whom God elected He draws to Himself through irresistible grace. God makes sinners willing to come to Him.

  • John 6:37 & 44: “No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws him, and I will raise him up at the last day.”
  • Galatians 3:14: “…so that by faith we might receive the promise of the Spirit.”
  • 2 Peter 1:3–4: “His divine power has given us everything we need for life and godliness… granted to us his precious and very great promises…”

None whom God elected will be lost; they are eternally secure in Christ.

  • John 10:28–29: “I give them eternal life, and they shall never perish; no one can snatch them out of my hand. My Father, who has given them to me, is greater than all; no one can snatch them out of my Father’s hand.”
  • Romans 8:1: “Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.”
  • Romans 8:31–39: “For I am convinced that neither death nor life… nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord.”
Historic Reformed Confessions

The Forms of Unity

In our Reformed churches we hold four Forms of Unity: the Heidelberg Catechism, the Belgic Confession, the Canons of Dort, and the Westminster Confession of Faith. We understand these confessions to be faithful summaries of what is taught in God’s Word.

Unlike Scripture — which stands supreme and cannot be challenged — the Forms of Unity are subordinate standards. Anyone who believes a confession expresses something contrary to Scripture may bring a ‘gravamen’ first to the local church elders, then to the Classis, and if necessary to the triennial Synod.

“What is thy only comfort in life and in death? That I, with body and soul, both in life and in death, am not my own, but belong to my faithful Savior Jesus Christ, who with His precious blood has fully satisfied for all my sins, and redeemed me from all the power of the devil…” Heidelberg Catechism · Lord’s Day 1, Question & Answer 1
1563 · Heidelberg

The Heidelberg Catechism

Composed in Heidelberg at the request of Elector Frederick III by Zacharias Ursinus and Caspar Olevianus and approved in January 1563. Divided into 52 Lord’s Days so one section can be preached each Sunday of the year, teaching our misery, redemption in Christ, and life of gratitude.

1561 · Netherlands

The Belgic Confession

Our oldest doctrinal standard, written primarily by Guido de Brès in 1561 amidst fierce persecution (before his martyrdom in 1567). Revised at Antwerp (1566) and ratified at the Synod of Dort (1618–1619), it sets out Christian doctrine across 37 articles.

1618–1619 · Dordrecht

The Canons of Dort

Formulated by the international Synod of Dordrecht (Nov 1618 – Apr 1619) in five chapters responding to the five Arminian Remonstrance articles — affirming total inability, unconditional election, Christ’s effective atonement, irresistible grace, and preservation in Christ.

1643–1648 · London

The Westminster Confession of Faith

Produced by the Assembly of Divines at Westminster Abbey (1643–1648) alongside the Larger and Shorter Catechisms, and adopted by the Reformed Churches of Australia in 1957 as our fourth confessional standard alongside the Belgic Confession.

Full Confessional Texts & Copies: Print copies of the Bible and the Heidelberg Catechism are available free upon request at church, and the full confessional texts are published via our denomination at crca.org.au.
